## Surface 03 — Manifesto section (§ FROM THE MANIFESTO)

### Current

> Most AI strategy hasn't been shipped.
>
> Most has never met a diligence partner. Most has never been edited by a board. Most was built in a slide deck by someone who has never been on the hook for the recommendation working at scale, under pressure, when an acquirer's counterparts (engineering, security, counsel) start pulling on threads.
>
> The pattern is structural, not personal.
>
> The operators who have actually shipped agentic AI into production at growth-stage scale, who have cleared adoption through real legal and IP review, who have led portfolios through public-company diligence: most stay in operating roles. The advisory market is mostly populated by people who looked at the work from a distance and turned it into slides.
>
> C9D Consulting is what we built when we left the operating seat.

### v2.2 (rewrite — opens on advising-vs-operating credential distinction with two axes: position and accountability duration)

> Advising and operating are different credentials.
>
> An advisor observes companies work through the wave — cloud, mobile, agentic AI — from outside, with accountability that ends when the SOW ends. An operator was inside a technology firm when the wave hit, accountable for the outcome long past when an advisor's engagement would have closed. Both credentials compound. They carry different kinds of judgment.
>
> C9D Consulting reasons from operating.
>
> Nearly two decades inside technology firms as engineer, architect, principal architect, and chief software architect — across enterprise software, technology & business strategy, governance, compliance, cybersecurity, mobile, SaaS, machine learning, agentic AI, and new product introduction. Not observed. Operated.
>
> The pattern is currently most visible in agentic AI. Most AI strategy hasn't been shipped. It was built in a slide deck, not in a production system, not under board scrutiny, not under the pressure of an acquirer's counterparts pulling on threads. Its authors have never been on the hook for the recommendation working. The advisory market for AI strategy is populated mostly by observers. Agentic AI is the current, most visible version of the pattern. The same dynamic shows up in cloud migration, in mobile, in SaaS adoption, in every cybersecurity inflection. It will show up in whatever cycle comes next.
>
> The pattern is structural, not personal.
>
> C9D Consulting is what we built from nearly two decades inside technology firms. Operator Advisory & Strategy for growth-stage decisions where complexity is real — whatever domain the decision actually lives in. Technology, when technology is the decision. Capital and portfolio work, when that is the decision. Governance, compliance, or operating cadence, when those are the decisions. The judgment that transfers is not any specific technology; it is the pattern of being inside the room when the cycle turned, and staying past when the observers closed their SOWs.

*Reason:* Two changes from v2.1. First, "observers" replaces "watchers" per vocabulary discipline (see SKILL.md invariant 20) — observers is more accurate and less pejorative. Second, the credential distinction now explicitly names both axes: **position** (inside vs outside the company) and **accountability duration** (SOW-bounded vs long-past-any-engagement). The SOW-boundedness point is the deeper half of the distinction — it explains *why* the credentials carry different judgment. An advisor's incentives run out with the invoice; an operator's don't.

**Rendering note for the site:** The named-arc list can render as either flowing prose (as above) or as a middle-dot-separated stat-strip in JetBrains Mono, dossier-style:

```
NEARLY TWO DECADES INSIDE TECHNOLOGY FIRMS:
ENTERPRISE SOFTWARE · TECHNOLOGY & BUSINESS STRATEGY · GOVERNANCE · COMPLIANCE · CYBERSECURITY · MOBILE · SAAS · MACHINE LEARNING · AGENTIC AI · NEW PRODUCT INTRODUCTION
```

Either treatment works. The stat-strip form matches The Dossier chrome pattern and scans faster for skim-reading; the prose form reads better for cover-to-cover.
