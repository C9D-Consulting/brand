## Contrarian belief

**[locked v2.3]** AI Strategy & Enablement is currently a hot advisory market populated mostly by observers — advisors whose credential comes from watching, whose accountability ends with the SOW. It is done well only by operators who have shipped AI in production, defended it under real diligence, and can draw on operator experience across the domains AI actually touches: cybersecurity, IP, governance, product introduction, capital events.

The corollary the market misses: an advisor whose entire credential is AI-observation has, by definition, not been on the hook for how AI decisions play out past their SOW. The AI wave is too new for observation-based credentials to have compounded into judgment.

## Primary alternative (the enemy)

**[locked v2.2]** **The advisor whose credential is observing, not operating.**

Both credentials compound. The advisor's compounds by observing many companies from outside — advising through a wave from a strategy firm, a research org, or an advisory-only role. Their accountability is bounded by the SOW; when it closes, they move to the next engagement. The operator's credential compounds by being inside a technology firm when the wave hit, accountable for the outcome long after any advisor's engagement would have closed. They carry different kinds of judgment. C9D Consulting reasons from the second.

The distinction is not that one is right and the other is wrong. It is that they are different tools for different decisions.

The v1.x primary alternative — "the AI strategist who has never shipped" — remains a specific instance of this pattern (someone advising on AI without operator experience in the AI wave). v2.2 generalizes: the advisor whose entire credential is observing, no matter how many cycles they observed from outside, has not operated. The two credentials differ on two axes — position (outside vs inside the company) and accountability duration (SOW-bounded vs long-past-any-engagement).

In archetype terms, this still positions against the Magician (the figure who turns work into slides) while conceding the Magician's craft is real. The concede-first move is load-bearing here — the brand does not need to attack observers to differentiate from them.

- **Proof artifact:** brandonwilburn.pro/case-studies/agentic-development

### RTB 3 — Stakes proof: Spirent → Keysight

Multi-year operator tenure at Spirent Communications. Four concurrent strategic initiatives ran toward a single acquisition-readiness mandate. Keysight acquisition close, October 2025 — fifteen percent premium over Viavi's competing bid. Zero critical vulnerabilities portfolio-wide at close. - **Maps to:** Posture & Diligence Review engagements, Operating Partner Retainer engagements for growth-to-exit trajectories.

## Four voice principles (unchanged)

**[locked]** Consistent across all contexts:

1. **Evidence-first.** Claims followed by proof artifacts. If a claim cannot be backed by an RTB or a documented case, it does not appear in copy.
2. **Plain-named-precision.** Name things specifically (Windsurf, Devin, DORA, Keysight, Spirent, $1.3B) rather than abstractly.
3. **Concede-first.** Acknowledge what's true in the opposing position before naming what's wrong.
4. **Diagnose-not-moralize.** Describe patterns and costs. Don't moralize about failures or the industry.
