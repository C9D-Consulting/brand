# Engagement Architecture — v2.0

The commercial structure of C9D Consulting. Two named engagements plus one retainer. Baseline-floor pricing. The architecture is locked; copy that describes services or pricing derives from this file.

**v2.0 change note:** Restructured from four AI-specific engagement types (v1.x: Productization, Agentic Adoption, Diligence Posture Review, Operating Partner Retainer) into three broader shapes (Decision Advisory, Posture & Diligence Review, Operating Partner Retainer). The shift matches the v2.0 positioning: engagements are shaped by *what kind of decision* the buyer faces, not by *what technology* the decision is about. AI is one domain within Decision Advisory; PE portfolio work, ops decisions, fractional CTO scoping, and technology inflection decisions are others.

---

## Service architecture

**[locked v2.0]** Three engagement shapes. Each carries the operator credential; each is priced at a baseline floor with no ceiling.

| ID | Name | Duration | Buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-01 | Decision Advisory | 30–120 days | CEO + functional lead (CTO / CFO / COO / board sponsor) |
| E-02 | Posture & Diligence Review | 30–60 days | CEO + CFO + board director (or investor sponsor) |
| R-01 | Operating Partner Retainer | 6–12 month minimum | CEO + board (or PE sponsor for portfolio engagements) |

### Four design rules for the service architecture (unchanged)

**[locked, updated for v2.0 scope]**

1. **Every engagement maps to operator credential.** No engagement shape exists that the operator cannot personally defend from prior operating experience (across cycles). The v1.x rule was "maps to a documented RTB"; v2.0 broadens to *operator credential*, which is a superset of the three documented RTBs.

2. **Engagements are sequential, not parallel.** One C9D Consulting engagement runs at a time. The "01 slot open" indicator is literal, not aspirational. This constrains revenue but protects the operator attention that makes each engagement work.

3. **The retainer is the exit pattern.** Engagements convert to retainers, not the other way around. A client does not start with R-01; they start with E-01 or E-02 and convert if the working relationship justifies an ongoing partnership.

4. **Refused engagement shapes (v2.0 update — see refuse list below for full):**
   - Hourly consulting / hourly advisory
   - "Workshops" or "training" as standalone products
   - Roadmap decks as the primary deliverable
   - Pure implementation work (the coder-in-the-sprint role)
   - Engagements that require guaranteeing an outcome the operator has not personally delivered before
   - Anything labeled "Phase 0 / Discovery" without a defined commercial commitment

## E-01 Decision Advisory

**[locked v2.0]** Absorbs and generalizes v1.x E-01 Productization, E-02 Agentic Adoption, and IP Posture Setup. Applies to any bounded, high-stakes decision at growth stage.

### Buyer
Growth-stage CEO or PE-sponsored portfolio company operator, working alongside the relevant functional lead (CTO for tech, CFO for capital, COO for ops, board sponsor for governance).

### Buyer's job-to-be-done (representative)

> *"We're facing a decision that will shape the next two board cycles or the next capital event. It's [technology adoption / capacity investment / build-vs-buy / restructure / market entry / vendor selection / capability productization]. Our internal thinking is real but not tested against operator judgment that's been through this shape of decision before. We need a diagnostic and a playbook, not a slide deck."*

### Scope
A bounded decision cycle. The engagement runs alongside the CEO and the named functional lead through a specific decision cycle: diagnostic against current state, playbook for the decision, handoff to the internal team that will execute.

Decision domains this engagement covers (non-exhaustive, illustrative):
- AI capability productization (v1.x E-01)
- Agentic development adoption (v1.x E-02)
- Enterprise IP posture (v1.x IP Posture Setup)
- Fractional CTO scoping for a specific technology inflection
- PE portfolio company operations review
- Omnichannel or growth-stage capacity decisions
- Build-vs-buy on major technology investments
- Vendor selection under diligence-grade constraints

### Deliverables (named, transferable)

The deliverable set adapts to the decision domain. Standard shape:

1. **Diagnostic Memo** (week 2–4): documented assessment of current state against the decision cycle.
2. **Decision Roadmap** (week 6–8): the recommended path with named stages, decision points, and kill criteria.
3. **Decision Record** (per phase): standing artifact of decisions made, rationale, and revision triggers.
4. **Playbook** (week 10–14): the re-runnable methodology the client's team owns after handoff.
5. **Handoff Documentation Pack** (final week): everything the internal team needs to continue without C9D Consulting.

Domain-specific deliverables (Vendor & Tooling Decision Record for tech decisions, Launch Instrumentation Playbook for capability productization, Seven-Dimension Review Findings for enterprise adoption, etc.) are subordinate to the standard shape.

### Duration
30–120 days depending on decision scope. Tighter, single-decision engagements: 30–60 days. Full multi-phase decision cycles: 90–120 days.

### Pricing
Fixed scope, fixed price. From $95,000 for single-decision engagements; from $185,000 for full-cycle multi-phase engagements.

### Qualification triggers (engagement is fit when)
- A specific decision is in front of the buyer, not a general strategy request
- The buyer is empowered to commit to the decision
- The decision cycle has a real forcing function (board, capital event, market timing, technology inflection)
- The operator has personally navigated a materially comparable decision before

### Disqualification triggers (decline when)
- No specific decision in view (this is "strategy consulting," not decision advisory)
- Buyer wants Brandon to make the decision instead of informing it
- Buyer wants implementation work (not the advisory role)
- Comparable decision falls outside operator experience

## E-02 Posture & Diligence Review

**[locked v2.0]** Preserves v1.x E-03 Diligence Posture Review concept, broadens beyond AI-specific diligence to full operating-posture review ahead of capital events, board reviews, or acquirer preparation.

### Buyer
CEO + CFO + a board director, or a PE sponsor arranging pre-diligence review of a portfolio company, at a company within 12 months of expected acquisition, fundraise, or significant strategic review.

### Buyer's job-to-be-done (v2.0, broadened)

> *"We're heading into [acquisition / Series D / strategic review / PE portfolio review] in the next year. Our operating story is going to get pulled on across technology, IP, security, operations, and financials. We don't know what we don't know — and we need someone who has been in the room when counterparts (engineering, security, counsel, finance, product) actually pull on these threads."*

### Scope
Stress-test the client's operating posture against actual M&A, fundraise, and PE diligence patterns. Coverage adapts to the buyer's specific exposure profile:

- Technology and product architecture claims survivability
- IP and inventorship documentation
- Security and license posture
- AI adoption exposure (from v1.x E-03; still a common thread)
- Vendor agentic tooling indemnity
- Customer contract clauses (AI, IP, exclusivity, MFN)
- Operating metrics defensibility
- Organizational scalability claims

Output is a remediation roadmap and a documented posture artifact the company can submit to diligence.

### Deliverables

1. **Posture Diagnostic** (week 2): comprehensive map of current diligence exposure across the covered dimensions.
2. **Remediation Roadmap** (week 4): prioritized fixes with effort, owner, and timing.
3. **Posture Artifact** (week 6): documented response to the 30–40 most common acquirer/investor questions across the covered dimensions.
4. **Handoff + Tabletop Walkthrough** (week 8): 90-minute working session with client's CFO, counsel, and board sponsor.

### Duration
30–60 days (or extended to 90 days with remediation oversight).

### Pricing
Fixed scope, fixed price. From $95,000. Highest absolute price relative to duration — reflects the stakes (deal close timing) and the scarcity of operators who've actually closed a $1.3B-class acquisition.

### Qualification triggers
- A specific event horizon is in view (LOI signed or expected, fundraise opened, PE review scheduled, board diligence incoming)
- The buyer group includes CEO + CFO minimum (posture reviews without CFO involvement don't work)
- The company has something to defend (not pure greenfield)

### Disqualification triggers
- No specific event horizon (this is strategy work, not posture review)
- Buyer wants Brandon to run the diligence process instead of preparing the company for it
- Coverage requires expertise entirely outside operator experience

## R-01 Operating Partner Retainer

**[locked v2.0, broadened]** Preserves v1.x R-01 with broader scope: applies to any operator-partner engagement, not just AI-specific ongoing work.

### Buyer
CEO + board, or PE sponsor for portfolio-level engagements.

### Pattern
The retainer is the **exit pattern** from a prior engagement. R-01 does not start cold. A client completes E-01 or E-02, and converts if the working relationship justifies an ongoing partnership.

### Scope (v2.0 additions in italics)
- Standing strategic partnership with the CEO
- Quarterly board-cycle preparation
- *PE portfolio operator role (advisor to portfolio CEO on behalf of PE sponsor)*
- *Fractional executive presence for a defined operating scope*
- Standing decision-cycle counsel

### Non-scope (unchanged)
- Day-to-day execution
- Direct management of any client employees
- Hands-on implementation
- Board seat (advisor to board is in scope; sitting on the board is not)

### Minimum commitment
6–12 months.

### Pricing
From $18,000/month. Fixed scope monthly retainer. PE portfolio-level retainers priced separately based on portfolio scope.

### Cadence
Weekly working session with named principal. Monthly strategic review. Quarterly outcomes review against pre-stated objectives.

## Refuse list — v2.0

**[locked v2.0]** The refusal culture is structural to the brand and unchanged. What changed: refuse *shapes of engagement*, not *domains of work*. Six refusals. Same count as v1.x, redistributed.

### Refusals kept from v1.x

- **Refusal 01. Write a roadmap deck as the primary deliverable.**
- **Refusal 02. Promise transformation.**
- **Refusal 03. Guarantee outcomes we have not personally delivered.**
- **Refusal 06. Tell you what we know before what we don't.**

### Refusal modified from v1.x

- **Refusal 04 (modified). Recommend approaches we cannot defend from operator experience.**
   - v1.x wording was "Recommend work we have not shipped." That refusal was too narrow — Brandon can defend approaches that were operator-tested even where specific artifacts weren't personally shipped. "Approaches we cannot defend from operator experience" preserves the bite, opens the aperture.

### Refusal replaced from v1.x

- **Refusal 05 (replaced). Take on pure implementation as the primary deliverable.**
   - v1.x wording was "Engage outside the five named verticals." That refusal was the single biggest door-closer and did not reflect the real practice. Replaced with an engagement-shape refusal: the practice is Operator Advisory & Strategy, not implementation. If the buyer wants a coder-in-the-sprint role, they need a different practice.

### Retired refusal (from live site)

- **"Engage outside the five named verticals."** Retired in v2.0. Replaced by Refusal 05 above.

## Qualification mechanics (v2.0 update)

**[locked v2.0]** Three-layer qualification system. Substantially unchanged from v1.x; the disqualifier list broadens as verticals retire.

### Layer 1 — Self-qualification (published-surface filter)
Website, copy, and pricing do this work without operator time. Published baselines ($95K, $185K, $18K/month) do most of Layer 1.

### Layer 2 — Pre-call qualification (5–10 minute exchange)
Establishes decision-in-view, buyer authority, and forcing function.

### Layer 3 — Diagnostic call qualification (20 minutes)
The intro call diagnoses fit. If no fit, ends as a referral.

### Cross-cutting hard disqualifiers (v2.0)

**[locked v2.0]**

1. No specific decision or forcing function in view (this is "strategy consulting," not decision advisory)
2. Buyer cannot meet baseline floor on the relevant engagement
3. Buyer wants hourly billing or rate-card pricing
4. The decision in front of them is a hire (the practice is not a recruiter)
5. The decision requires pure implementation as the primary deliverable
6. Direct conflict of interest with an existing engagement
7. The decision falls entirely outside operator experience (across cycles, not just current cycle)
8. Buyer wants Brandon to make the decision or run the process, not inform it

Notable removal from v1.x: "Outside the named verticals" is no longer a disqualifier. Vertical fit is not the filter; decision shape and operator-experience defensibility are.
